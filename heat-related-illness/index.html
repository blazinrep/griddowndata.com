Heat Exhaustion and Heat Stroke: Emergency First Aid

Heat stroke is an emergency. Confusion, collapse or altered consciousness after heat exposure warrants calling 911 and starting rapid cooling immediately, without waiting for a thermometer reading. Heat exhaustion also requires removal from heat, cooling and monitoring.

During a summer power outage, plan access to a cooler location and check vulnerable people. Fans alone may not provide adequate cooling in extreme heat.

Frequently asked questions

How do I tell heat exhaustion from heat stroke?

Heat exhaustion can cause heavy sweating, weakness, nausea and dizziness. Suspect heat stroke if mental status changes, seizures or collapse occur after heat exposure, whether skin is dry or sweaty. Do not wait for a specific temperature.

What is the first aid for heat stroke?

Call 911 and start rapid cooling immediately. Move to a cooler location, remove excess clothing and use cold-water immersion if feasible and safe; otherwise wet the skin, fan and use cold wet cloths. Monitor breathing and do not give drinks to someone confused or unable to swallow.

How do I treat heat exhaustion before it gets worse?

Move the person to a cool place, loosen clothing and cool with wet cloths. Offer small sips of water only if fully alert and able to swallow. Get medical help if vomiting, symptoms worsen or fail to improve promptly; confusion or collapse requires 911.

How can I prevent heat illness when there is no air conditioning?

Reduce strenuous activity, take frequent cooling breaks, wear light clothing and drink water regularly. Seek air-conditioned shelter during dangerous heat and check people at higher risk.
