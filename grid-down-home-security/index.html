When response times stretch to never, your home's defense is whatever you built beforehand.

Grid-down home security is protecting a household when police response is delayed or absent, using layered defense: deterrence, perimeter barriers, detection, and structural hardening. It covers clear sightlines, fencing and gates, motion lights, sound-amplifying gravel, reinforced doors and deadbolts on first-floor entry points, and operational security to avoid advertising resources. Frequently asked questions

What does layered home defense mean?

Layered defense stacks independent measures so that defeating one still leaves others in the intruder's way, much like the layers of an onion. The outer layer is deterrence and sightlines, then perimeter fencing and gates, then detection such as lights and cameras, and finally structural hardening of the home itself. The guide explains building each layer so it reinforces the ones around it.

Which entry points should I harden first?

First-floor doors and windows are by far the most common points of forced entry, so they come first. Hardening focuses on where the lock and hinges attach to the frame, since that is what fails under force. The guide covers replacing hollow doors with solid reinforced ones, adding deadbolts and door jammers, and reinforcing strike plates and window locks against prying.

What is OPSEC and why does it matter in a crisis?

Operational security, or OPSEC, means not advertising the resources, supplies, or capabilities that would make you a target. In a prolonged emergency, visible generators, lights at night, cooking smells, and loose talk can all draw attention. The guide covers light and scent discipline, concealing high-value stores, and controlling what neighbors and passersby can observe about your preparations.

How can I get early warning of intruders off-grid?

Detection buys the seconds needed to respond. Motion-activated lights, gravel or rock beds that crunch loudly underfoot, dogs, and simple mechanical alarms all signal movement without depending on the grid. Combined with clear sightlines and signage that establishes a territorial boundary, these measures alert you before someone reaches the house. The guide details low-power and no-power early-warning options.
