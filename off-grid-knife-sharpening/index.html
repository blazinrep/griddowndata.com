Frequently asked questions

Why does a sharp edge matter so much off-grid?

A dull blade forces you to push harder, which tires you faster and makes the tool far more likely to slip and cause a deep cut, exactly the injury you cannot afford when medical help is distant. A sharp knife processes game, prepares food, and cuts cordage cleanly and with control, so keeping an edge is a genuine safety and efficiency skill rather than a chore.

What angle should I hold when sharpening a knife?

Most general-purpose knives take a bevel of roughly seventeen to twenty degrees per side, while axes and choppers use a steeper, more durable angle and fine slicing knives a shallower one. The guide explains finding and holding a consistent angle by feel, using a simple angle guide, and why keeping the angle steady matters more than hitting an exact number for producing a lasting edge.

What tools do I need to sharpen without electricity?

A basic kit is a combination coarse-and-fine sharpening stone or a diamond plate, a flat file for axes and machetes, and a leather strop for finishing. The guide covers using water or oil on stones, keeping the stone flat as it wears hollow, and improvising with a smooth river rock or the unglazed base of a ceramic mug when no proper stone is available.

What is a burr and why does it matter?

A burr is the thin ridge of metal that folds over to the opposite side of the edge once you have ground far enough on one side, and feeling it confirms you have reached the very edge. The guide explains detecting the burr with a light fingertip drag, flipping the blade to raise it on the other side, then removing it with light alternating finishing strokes and stropping for a clean, keen edge.
