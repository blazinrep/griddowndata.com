Winter greenhouse growing is producing fresh vegetables through the cold months in an unheated or minimally heated structure that traps solar heat, raising the interior temperature enough that cold-hardy crops survive freezes that would kill them outdoors. Kale, spinach, carrots, leeks, and similar hardy crops keep yielding, and a greenhouse bed of carrots under straw becomes a self-replenishing root cellar. Frequently asked questions

How does an unheated greenhouse keep plants alive in freezing weather?

Sunlight passes through the translucent covering and is trapped inside as heat, raising the interior temperature by roughly ten degrees Fahrenheit and buffering the sharp swings that kill plants. In practice a greenhouse shifts you about one climate zone warmer, so a crop hardy to thirty degrees can survive outdoor lows near twenty. The guide explains adding a layer of floating row cover inside the greenhouse to gain still more protection on the coldest nights.

What vegetables grow best in a winter greenhouse?

Cold-hardy leafy greens such as kale, spinach, chard, arugula, and mache lead the list, alongside root crops like carrots, beets, and turnips and alliums like leeks and scallions. Many brassicas actually turn sweeter after a frost. The guide groups crops by cold hardiness and by calorie value, so you can balance quick-growing greens against the denser root vegetables that carry more real nutrition through the winter months.

When should I plant for a winter harvest?

Timing is decisive: cold-season crops must be sown in late summer or early autumn so they reach near-full size before daylight drops below roughly ten hours, after which growth nearly stops and the greenhouse mainly holds mature plants in stasis for harvest. The guide provides a sow-by calendar keyed to your first frost date, so plants are well established rather than struggling when true winter cold and short days finally arrive.

Do winter greenhouse crops need extra protection on the coldest nights?

Often yes. On nights well below the greenhouse's normal buffer, a second layer such as floating row cover, a low tunnel, or mulch heaped over root crops traps ground warmth and shields foliage, and this double-layering can carry hardy crops through extreme cold with no fuel at all. The guide details these cheap passive measures plus venting on sunny days, since an unattended winter greenhouse can overheat and cook the very plants it protects.
