Field Notes / 001 · Stories of self-reliance

Two boys. Two horses.
One extraordinary ride.

In 1910, a ten-year-old and his six-year-old brother rode from Oklahoma to New York City — without an adult accompanying them on horseback.

A verified story from the American frontier era

OKLAHOMA · 1910 · NEW YORK CITYHorseback east.
Automobile west.LOUIS “BUD” & TEMPLE ABERNATHY

Imagine being six years old, swinging into the saddle, and setting off across the country with your ten-year-old brother. No parent riding alongside. No cell phone. No modern navigation app.

That was the spring of 1910 for Temple Abernathy and his older brother, Louis “Bud” Abernathy, who left the area around Frederick, Oklahoma, to greet former President Theodore Roosevelt upon his return from travels in Africa and Europe.

Their father, Jack “Catch-’em-alive” Abernathy, was no ordinary cowboy. Known for capturing wolves alive with his hands, Jack had hosted Roosevelt on a wolf hunt in Oklahoma Territory in 1905. Roosevelt later appointed him a United States marshal.

This wasn't their first long ride

In 1909, the brothers had already ridden horseback from Oklahoma to Santa Fe, New Mexico, and back, when Bud was nine and Temple five. It was remarkable preparation for an even greater journey.

In 1910 they rode east. Newspapers followed their progress; curious crowds turned out to meet them. Their route took them through Washington, D.C., before they reached New York in June.

In New York, they joined the public celebration welcoming Theodore Roosevelt home.

On June 18, the brothers rode their horses in Roosevelt's welcome-home parade. The boys who had crossed the country under their own power had become national celebrities.

And then they drove home

The return trip might be the strangest part of the story. Rather than ride their horses west, the family arranged to send them home by rail. The boys traveled back in a small Brush Runabout automobile, with their father following separately in a Maxwell touring car, according to the local historical marker account.

Two boys had crossed an enormous stretch of America on horseback — and returned behind the wheel of a machine that still seemed futuristic in many towns they passed.

What the story leaves us with

This wasn't an ordinary childhood, even in 1910, and the journey carried real risks. But it's a remarkable example of confidence, horsemanship, determination, and the way an adventure can capture a nation's imagination.
