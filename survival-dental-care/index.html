Frequently asked questions

What goes in an emergency dental kit?

A field dental kit typically includes temporary filling material such as Dentemp, clove oil or benzocaine gel for pain, dental wax, cotton pellets, tweezers, a small mirror, gauze for bleeding, and floss. Together these let you temporarily manage the most common field problems: toothaches, lost fillings, broken teeth, and post-extraction bleeding, until you reach a dentist. The guide details assembling a compact kit that fits in a small pouch or aid bag.

How do I stop a toothache without a dentist?

Start by rinsing with warm salt water to clean the area, then hold a cold compress against the outside of the jaw for swelling and take an over-the-counter pain reliever as directed. Clove oil, a traditional dental analgesic containing eugenol, can be dabbed onto the sore tooth for relief. These measures ease pain temporarily but do not cure the underlying problem, which still needs professional treatment as soon as it is reachable.

Can I replace a lost filling or crown myself?

Temporarily, yes. Over-the-counter temporary filling material or dental cement can protect an exposed tooth or re-seat a loose crown for a short period, easing sensitivity and keeping the cavity clean. The guide explains cleaning and drying the tooth first, mixing and placing the material, and gently biting to set it. This is strictly a short-term fix meant to hold until a dentist can examine and properly restore the tooth.

When does a dental problem become a real emergency?

Warning signs of a serious dental infection include spreading facial swelling, fever, difficulty swallowing or breathing, and pain radiating into the jaw or neck. An abscess can spread into deeper tissue and become dangerous, so these symptoms mean you should reach professional care as urgently as possible. The guide helps you distinguish a manageable toothache from a spreading infection that must not simply be waited out at home.
