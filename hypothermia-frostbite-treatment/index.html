Hypothermia and Frostbite: Emergency First Aid

Hypothermia and frostbite are potentially serious cold injuries. For suspected hypothermia, call 911, move the person out of the cold, remove wet clothing and insulate them. Handle gently and avoid direct heat or rubbing. Frostbite needs medical evaluation; do not thaw tissue if it might refreeze.

Cold injuries can develop indoors during outages as well as outdoors. Learn the warning signs and prepare dry insulation and a way to contact emergency services.

Frequently asked questions

How do I recognize hypothermia?

Shivering, clumsiness, confusion, slurred speech and drowsiness can signal hypothermia. Shivering may stop as the condition worsens. Do not wait for a thermometer reading or loss of consciousness to seek emergency help.

What is the right way to rewarm someone?

Move to shelter, remove wet clothing and insulate with dry blankets, including the head and neck. Warm the trunk gently with wrapped warm packs, not bare hot objects. Avoid warm baths, rubbing and vigorous movement in moderate or severe hypothermia. Give nothing by mouth if not fully alert and able to swallow.

How should frostbite be handled?

Protect frostbitten skin from pressure and further cold. Do not rub, use direct heat or thaw if refreezing is possible. If medical help is delayed and refreezing can be prevented, gently warm in clean water at 99–104°F (37–40°C). Seek medical care and do not break blisters.

When is a cold injury a medical emergency?

Call 911 for suspected hypothermia, altered consciousness or significant cold injury. If the person is not breathing normally, follow dispatcher instructions for CPR and AED use. Seek prompt medical evaluation for frostbite.
