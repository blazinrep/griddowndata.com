Frequently asked questions

How long does stored gasoline last?

Untreated gasoline typically stays usable for only about a year before it oxidizes and gums up, and ethanol blends absorb water faster still. Adding a fuel stabilizer within that first year and remixing extends life, but stabilizer must generally be reapplied annually rather than just once. The guide covers stabilizer types, dosing, and how to rotate gasoline through your vehicles before it degrades.

How long can diesel be stored?

Diesel generally lasts eighteen to twenty-four months in cool, dry, sealed tanks, but modern ultra-low-sulfur diesel may not last much past six months untreated. With stabilizers, biocides, and water monitoring it can be extended for years, especially in large, well-inspected tanks. The reference explains treating diesel, preventing microbial growth, and checking for the water buildup that causes most storage failures.

Why do stored fuels need stabilizers and biocides?

Fuel degrades through oxidation, evaporation of light components, and, in diesel, microbial growth at the fuel-water interface. Stabilizers slow oxidation and gum formation while biocides kill the bacteria and fungi that clog filters and corrode tanks. The guide details which additives address which problem, how often to reapply them, and how sealed, cool, dark storage multiplies their effect on shelf life.

How should I rotate a fuel supply safely?

Rotation means using older stored fuel before it degrades and replacing it with fresh, typically cycling gasoline within a year and diesel within one to five years depending on treatment. Pouring stored gasoline into your daily vehicle and refilling the container keeps stock fresh. The guide provides labeling, dating, and rotation schedules plus safe container and ventilation practices to reduce fire risk.
