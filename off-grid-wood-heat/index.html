Off-Grid Wood Heat: Safe Stove and Backup Planning

A properly installed wood stove can supply heat during outages. Safe planning includes correct sizing, dry fuel, listed chimney components, required clearances and working smoke and carbon monoxide alarms.

Have an experienced professional inspect and install wood-burning equipment. Never substitute outdoor cooking equipment or unvented improvised combustion for indoor heating.

Frequently asked questions

How much firewood do I need for a winter?

Firewood needs vary considerably by climate, heat loss, stove efficiency and fuel moisture. Estimate from local usage and a heat-loss assessment rather than a fixed number of cords.

How do I size a wood stove to my space?

Match the stove's BTU rating to your square footage, insulation level, and winter severity — an oversized stove overheats and runs inefficiently, an undersized one never keeps up.

How do I burn a wood stove safely?

Follow the stove listing and manufacturer clearances, have the chimney professionally inspected and cleaned as needed, burn dry wood and maintain smoke and carbon monoxide alarms. Never use accelerants.

Why plan more than one heat source?

Keep a safe backup heat plan and a way to relocate to a heated shelter. Use only heaters approved for their intended indoor location; do not seal required combustion air or ventilation.
