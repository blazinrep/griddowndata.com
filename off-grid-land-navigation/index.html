Frequently asked questions

What is magnetic declination and why does it matter?

Declination is the angular gap between true north on your map and the magnetic north your compass needle follows, and it varies by location. Ignoring it is the most common serious navigation mistake because the error compounds with distance, roughly 270 meters off per kilometer for a 15-degree offset. The reference shows how to set declination on a baseplate compass and verify it early.

How does pace counting measure distance without GPS?

Pace counting tracks how many double-steps you take to cover ground, calibrated ahead of time by walking a known distance and counting. Recording your cumulative count at each landmark lets you estimate how far you have traveled and know when to expect a turn or feature. The guide includes calibration methods and worksheets to record your personal pace across varied terrain.

What is terrain association?

Terrain association is matching visible ground features such as ridges, valleys, stream bends, and saddles to their contour-line equivalents on a topographic map, rather than relying only on strict compass bearings. Experienced navigators prefer it because it is faster and self-correcting. The offline reference teaches reading contour lines, identifying handrails to follow, and cross-checking your position against the landscape around you.
