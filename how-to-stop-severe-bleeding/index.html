How to Stop Severe Bleeding: Emergency First Aid

Life-threatening bleeding needs immediate action. Call 911 or have someone call while you apply firm direct pressure. Use a commercial tourniquet promptly for life-threatening arm or leg bleeding; pack appropriate deep wounds only if trained. Do not loosen a tourniquet after application.

Keep gloves, gauze and a reliable commercial tourniquet accessible, and practice with a qualified instructor. This overview is not a substitute for emergency care or hands-on training.

Frequently asked questions

How do I stop severe bleeding step by step?

Make sure the scene is safe and call 911. Apply steady, firm pressure directly to the bleeding site. Do not remove the original dressing to check. For severe limb bleeding, use a tourniquet promptly. If trained, pack appropriate deep external wounds; never pack an open chest or abdominal cavity.

When should I use a tourniquet?

For life-threatening bleeding from an arm or leg, apply a commercial tourniquet promptly, about 2–3 inches above the wound and not over a joint. Tighten until bleeding stops. Follow device instructions and dispatcher guidance; if bleeding continues, a second tourniquet may be needed above the first.

Do I need to write down the time I applied a tourniquet?

Record the application time and tell emergency responders. Do not periodically loosen, remove or cover the tourniquet. Leave removal to medical professionals.

What can I use if I do not have a commercial tourniquet?

A commercial tourniquet is preferred. If none is available, maintain uninterrupted direct pressure and use wound packing if trained and appropriate. Follow emergency dispatcher instructions; do not delay pressure while searching for equipment.

Can I control bleeding with pressure points or elevation instead?

Do not delay direct pressure or a tourniquet to try elevation or pressure points. Keep the person warm, monitor breathing and follow emergency dispatcher instructions until help arrives.
