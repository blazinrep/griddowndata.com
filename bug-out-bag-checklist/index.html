Bug Out Bag Checklist for a 72-Hour Evacuation

A bug out bag is a portable emergency kit for evacuating to safety. Pack water, food, weather protection, first aid, prescriptions, lighting, navigation and important documents. Adjust the load to your physical ability and evacuation plan.

Keep essential supplies accessible and practice carrying the bag. A 72-hour kit is a starting point, not a guarantee of safety.

Frequently asked questions

What exactly is a bug out bag?

A bug out bag is a portable kit, usually a backpack, holding the gear and supplies one person needs to survive for roughly seventy-two hours away from home. Its purpose is deliberately short-term: to carry you through the first three days of an evacuation while you reach a safer, longer-term location. It is grab-and-go, kept fully packed and accessible rather than assembled during the emergency itself when there is no time.

How much should a bug out bag weigh?

Choose a weight you can carry safely over the terrain you expect, accounting for health, mobility and water needs. A percentage of body weight is only a rough planning heuristic, not a universal target.

Which categories are most essential to pack?

Prioritize safe drinking water, food, shelter, medications, first aid, communication and essential documents. A filter alone may not remove viruses or chemicals; use appropriate water treatment.

How is a bug out bag different from a get-home bag?

A bug out bag is built to evacuate you from home for about three days, while a get-home bag is a smaller kit kept in your vehicle or office to get you back home on foot if transport and communication fail. They overlap but differ in size and goal.
