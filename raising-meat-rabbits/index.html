Frequently asked questions

Which rabbit breeds are best for meat?

Medium-to-large meat breeds win: New Zealand Whites and Californians are the standards, growing to 9 to 11 pounds and reaching a 5-pound fryer weight in about ten weeks. Avoid small and dwarf breeds, which never put on enough meat to justify their feed. The guide compares breeds by growth rate, litter size, temperament, and heat tolerance so you match stock to your climate.

How fast do meat rabbits reach butcher weight?

Most meat breeds hit a live weight of 4 to 5 pounds in roughly 8 to 12 weeks, yielding about 2 to 3.5 pounds of dressed meat per fryer. Because a doe can be rebred while her litter grows out, a staggered schedule delivers a fresh batch every few weeks. The guide lays out breeding and grow-out timelines for continuous, year-round production.

Why are rabbits so feed-efficient?

Rabbits convert roughly 3 to 4 pounds of feed into one pound of body weight, nearly twice as efficient as beef cattle, and they thrive partly on forage, garden trimmings, and hay rather than only bagged pellets. That efficiency, combined with year-round breeding, makes them one of the most productive protein sources a small property can support. The guide covers balancing forage with stored feed.

How do I protect and house meat rabbits?

Rabbits need dry, shaded, well-ventilated hutches raised off the ground, with wire floors for sanitation and secure latches against raccoons, dogs, and weasels. Heat is a bigger killer than cold, so shade and airflow matter most in summer. The guide details hutch dimensions, predator-proofing, nest boxes for kindling does, and winter wind protection so your breeding herd stays healthy and productive.
