How to Make Pemmican: Ingredients and Food Safety

Pemmican is a traditional mixture of dried lean meat and rendered fat. Its high calorie density is useful, but a homemade mixture is not automatically safe for unrefrigerated storage. Follow validated meat-drying guidance and refrigerate or freeze the finished mixture.

Pemmican has a long Indigenous history and was adopted by travelers and fur traders. Modern home preparation should not assume that historical storage claims apply to an untested batch.

Frequently asked questions

What is the correct fat-to-meat ratio for pemmican?

Traditional recipes often use similar weights of dried meat and rendered fat. The ratio affects texture and energy density; it does not establish microbial safety or shelf life.

How do I render fat into tallow for pemmican?

Rendering separates fat from meat and water. Use clean ingredients and equipment, avoid splattering hot fat, and cool and refrigerate or freeze the finished product. Appearance alone does not establish safety.

How long does homemade pemmican last?

There is no validated room-temperature shelf-life claim for the homemade mixture described here. Treat it as perishable, refrigerate promptly at 40°F or below and freeze for longer storage. Do not use oxygen absorbers to make an unvalidated meat-and-fat mixture shelf-stable.

What meat and fat should I avoid using for pemmican?

Use lean meat from a safe source. USDA recommends heating meat to 160°F and poultry to 165°F before drying for jerky. Follow validated guidance for the species and process; drying alone is not a reliable pathogen kill step.
