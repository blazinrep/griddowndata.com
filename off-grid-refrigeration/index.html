Off-Grid Refrigeration and Safe Food Storage

Passive cooling can help certain whole fruits and vegetables but is not automatically safe refrigeration. Perishable foods require verified temperatures of 40°F (4°C) or below. Use a thermometer and follow outage food safety rules.

Root cellars, evaporative pots and spring boxes vary with climate and contamination risk. A properly installed refrigerator or ice-filled cooler with monitored temperatures is a better option for perishables.

Frequently asked questions

How does a zeer pot keep food cold?

A zeer pot uses evaporation through porous clay to cool its contents. Performance varies greatly with humidity and airflow. Use it for suitable whole produce, not meat, dairy, cooked leftovers or other perishables unless safe refrigeration temperature is continuously verified.

Does a zeer pot work in humid climates?

High humidity limits evaporation. Do not assume a cellar or spring box will compensate; measure temperature and prevent contamination. Use a reliable refrigerator or ice-filled cooler for perishables.

How does a root cellar refrigerate food?

A root cellar may suit some whole storage crops, with conditions tailored to each crop. It is not a substitute for a refrigerator for meat, milk, eggs or leftovers.

What about propane or absorption refrigerators?

Propane absorption refrigerators can provide refrigeration if correctly installed, ventilated and monitored. Keep food at 40°F or below; follow the appliance instructions and medication-specific storage instructions.

Can't keep the meat cold? Preserve it instead — see Curing & Smoking Meat for safe preservation methods and their limits.
