Frequently asked questions

How does a zeer pot keep food cold?

A zeer pot nests a smaller clay pot inside a larger one with wet sand packed between them; as water evaporates from the outer surface it draws heat away, chilling the inner chamber. In dry, breezy shade it can hold temperatures roughly 8 to 18°C below the surrounding air, extending the life of vegetables that would otherwise spoil in days. The guide details construction and daily watering.

Does a zeer pot work in humid climates?

Not well. Evaporative coolers depend on dry air pulling moisture away, so in high humidity evaporation slows sharply and cooling drops off. The guide is honest about this limit and points humid-climate users toward root cellars, spring boxes, and propane or absorption refrigerators instead, matching the method to your actual climate rather than promising a single solution works everywhere.

How does a root cellar refrigerate food?

A root cellar uses the stable temperature of the earth, being partly or fully buried so it stays cool in summer and above freezing in winter. It suits dense storage crops like carrots, potatoes, onions, and apples that keep for months in cool, humid, dark conditions. The guide covers siting, ventilation, humidity control, and which foods store well together without spoiling one another.

What about propane or absorption refrigerators?

Absorption refrigerators use a heat source such as propane instead of a compressor and electricity, making them a practical off-grid option for true cold storage of meat, dairy, and medicine. The guide compares their fuel use, capacity, and ventilation needs against passive methods, helping you decide when the fuel cost is worth reliable refrigeration versus when evaporative or cellar cooling will suffice.
