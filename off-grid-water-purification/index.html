Frequently asked questions

What water purification methods are covered?

The reference covers filtration to remove sediment, protozoa, and bacteria; boiling to kill microorganisms; chemical treatment with chlorine or iodine; and safe storage to prevent recontamination. It explains what each method does and does not handle, so you can combine them — for example filtering cloudy water before boiling — to make questionable sources reliably safe to drink.

How long should I boil water to make it safe?

Bringing water to a rolling boil for one minute kills the bacteria, viruses, and protozoa that cause most waterborne illness, and at high altitude the guide advises boiling for three minutes because water boils at a lower temperature. Boiling does not remove chemical contaminants or sediment, so the reference pairs it with filtration when needed.

Does filtering remove viruses?

Most portable filters remove bacteria and protozoa like Giardia and Cryptosporidium but not viruses, which are smaller than the filter pores. The guide explains this limit and recommends adding boiling or chemical disinfection when viruses are a concern, such as with water contaminated by sewage. Matching the method to the threat is the core of safe treatment.

Do I need internet to use it?

No. All references are stored on the air-gapped drive and work fully offline on a phone, tablet, or laptop. Because purifying water becomes critical precisely when utilities and networks fail, the guidance is built to be available with no signal or power during outages, in the backcountry, or in the aftermath of a disaster.
