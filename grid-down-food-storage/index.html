Feeding your family for months is a skill set. Get it offline.

Grid-down food storage is the practice of growing, preserving, and rotating food so a household can eat for months without resupply or refrigeration. It spans safe home canning, dehydrating, root-cellar construction, curing and smoking, and disciplined rotation of long-term staples like grains, legumes, and fats. GridDownData provides these offline guides on an air-gapped USB drive that works with no power or internet, so the knowledge is there when supply chains stall.

Frequently asked questions

How much food-storage knowledge is included?

GridDownData includes field-tested guides for gardening, safe home canning, dehydrating, curing and smoking, root-cellar construction, and long-term staple rotation. Together they cover producing, preserving, and storing food across seasons rather than a single method, so a household can build real food security instead of relying on a short-term stockpile. Everything is stored offline on the drive.

Does it work during a power outage?

Yes. The drive is air-gapped and fully offline, so every guide is available even when the grid and cell networks are down. That matters because a prolonged outage is exactly when you need to preserve food without refrigeration, using methods like canning, curing, and root-cellar storage that the reference explains in detail.

Is home canning safe if done from a guide?

Home canning is safe when you follow tested procedures, and the reference stresses the fundamentals: using a pressure canner for low-acid foods to prevent botulism, correct processing times and jar handling, and discarding anything with a failed seal. It teaches the reasoning behind each rule, and it supplements, not replaces, established food-safety guidance.

What foods store the longest without power?

Dry staples such as white rice, wheat, rolled oats, dried beans, sugar, salt, and honey can last years when sealed against moisture, oxygen, and pests. The guide explains packaging with Mylar and oxygen absorbers, ideal cool and dark storage conditions, and how to rotate stock so your oldest supplies get used first and nothing is wasted.
