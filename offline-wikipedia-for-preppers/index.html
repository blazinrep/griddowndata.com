The internet you saved for later.

An offline copy of Wikipedia is stored as a ZIM file — a compressed archive of the site's articles — and opened with Kiwix, a free reader available on every major platform. No account, no internet connection, no subscription. A text-only English archive runs roughly 50–90 GB, small enough to sit on the same USB drive as a medical reference, water guide, and offline maps.

Most survival USB drives are built around a handful of curated PDFs: a medical manual, a water purification guide, maybe a repair reference. That covers the scenarios you thought to prepare for. Offline Wikipedia covers everything else — plant identification, basic chemistry, mechanical repair, geography, history — the questions you can't predict in advance.

How it actually works

The Kiwix project maintains ZIM downloads of Wikipedia, WikiHow, Project Gutenberg, and dozens of other reference sites, updated periodically and hosted for free public download. Once downloaded, the ZIM file and the Kiwix reader sit on the same drive — plug in, open Kiwix, point it at the file, and search or browse exactly like the live site.

Frequently asked questions

How do I get an offline copy of Wikipedia?

Download a Wikipedia ZIM file — a compressed archive of Wikipedia's articles — and open it with Kiwix, a free reader available for Windows, Mac, Linux, iOS, and Android. No account, no internet connection, and no ongoing subscription required once the file is downloaded.

What is a ZIM file?

A ZIM file is a compressed archive format built specifically for offline wikis. It packages the full text (and optionally images) of a site like Wikipedia or WikiHow into a single searchable file that the Kiwix reader opens instantly, without unpacking or converting anything.

How big is an offline Wikipedia download?

A text-only English Wikipedia ZIM file runs roughly 50–90 GB depending on the version and update date. Adding images pushes that to several hundred GB. Most preppers use the text-only version, which fits comfortably on a 128GB drive alongside a curated PDF library.

Is offline Wikipedia actually reliable for survival information?

It's a general-purpose reference, not a substitute for a dedicated medical or first-aid manual — use it alongside topic-specific guides, not instead of them. Its real value is breadth: it can answer thousands of specific questions no single curated PDF library will ever cover, from plant identification to basic mechanical repair.

Can I search an offline Wikipedia archive like the real site?

Yes. Kiwix indexes the full ZIM archive on first load, so searching, following internal links between articles, and browsing by category all work the same way they do on wikipedia.org — just without needing a connection.
