Off-Grid Hygiene: Staying Clean During Emergencies

Personal hygiene during an outage relies on safe water, soap, clean clothing and careful handling of waste. Prioritize handwashing before handling food and after using the toilet. Do not reuse contaminated wash water for handwashing.

Store soap, clean water containers and basic hygiene supplies before an emergency. Use official emergency water guidance for water safety.

Frequently asked questions

How is hygiene different from sanitation in a crisis?

Sanitation is the safe disposal of human waste, while hygiene is keeping your body, clothing, and surroundings clean, and the two work together. Even a well-managed latrine will not protect you if unwashed hands carry germs to food. This guide focuses on personal cleanliness — bathing, soap, laundry, and skin and oral care — and pairs with a separate reference dedicated to waste disposal and latrines.

How can I bathe with very little water?

Use safe water, soap and a clean cloth for a sponge bath. Avoid sharing dirty wash water or towels, particularly when someone is ill. Keep wounds away from contaminated water.

How do I make soap without buying it?

Homemade lye can cause serious chemical burns and its concentration is unpredictable. Stock ordinary soap instead; do not attempt ash-lye soap production without validated instructions, proper protective equipment and training.

How do I do laundry by hand off-grid?

Wash with soap and suitable water, rinse and dry fully. Handle soiled laundry separately and follow appropriate cleaning instructions for contaminated clothing. Sun drying alone is not a reliable disinfection process.

Clean hands and body are only half of staying healthy — safe waste disposal is the other half: see Off-Grid Sanitation.
