When the water stops running, staying clean is how you keep infection out of your camp.

Off-grid hygiene is keeping your body, clothing, and living space clean without running water or electricity, which prevents the skin infections, lice, and gastrointestinal illness that spread quickly once normal washing stops. It covers bathing with minimal water, making soap from fat and lye, hand-washing and drying laundry, and basic oral and skin care. GridDownData stores these methods on an air-gapped USB drive that works with no power or signal. It supplements, not replaces, professional medical care where that care can be reached.

Clean water for drinking gets all the attention, but in a prolonged emergency it is dirty hands, unwashed clothes, and untreated skin that quietly spread disease through a household. GridDownData carries offline references for staying clean when the taps are dry: bathing with a basin and a quart of water, rendering fat and leaching lye to make real soap, washing and drying laundry by hand, and keeping teeth and skin healthy. Frequently asked questions

How is hygiene different from sanitation in a crisis?

Sanitation is the safe disposal of human waste, while hygiene is keeping your body, clothing, and surroundings clean, and the two work together. Even a well-managed latrine will not protect you if unwashed hands carry germs to food. This guide focuses on personal cleanliness — bathing, soap, laundry, and skin and oral care — and pairs with a separate reference dedicated to waste disposal and latrines.

How can I bathe with very little water?

A full shower is a luxury off-grid, but you can stay clean with a sponge bath using about a quart of warm water, a cloth, and soap, washing the highest-odor and highest-risk areas first. The guide covers heating small amounts of water efficiently, using a solar shower bag on sunny days, and rinsing so that a single container of water can serve an entire family.

How do I make soap without buying it?

Traditional soap is made by combining a fat or oil with lye, which can be leached from hardwood ashes and water, then cooking and curing the mixture until it is safe to use. The guide explains handling caustic lye safely, measuring the fat-to-lye ratio, and the difference between soft potash soap and harder bar soap, so you can produce a real cleaning agent from materials on hand.

How do I do laundry by hand off-grid?

Hand laundry uses two tubs, one to wash with soap and agitation and one to rinse, plus a plunger-style agitator or a washboard to work grime out of the fabric. The guide covers wringing water out efficiently, line-drying in sunlight that helps disinfect, and prioritizing socks and underclothes, since damp, dirty fabric worn against skin causes rashes and infections fast.

Clean hands and body are only half of staying healthy — safe waste disposal is the other half: see Off-Grid Sanitation.
