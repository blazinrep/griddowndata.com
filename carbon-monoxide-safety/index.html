Carbon Monoxide Safety During Power Outages

Carbon monoxide is an invisible, odorless gas from burning fuel. It can cause serious illness or death. Never use generators, grills or outdoor-only fuel appliances indoors. If an alarm sounds or poisoning is suspected, leave immediately for fresh air and call 911 from outside.

Prevent carbon monoxide exposure before an outage with battery-backed alarms and safe outdoor generator placement. Do not reenter a suspected contaminated building until emergency personnel say it is safe.

Frequently asked questions

What are the symptoms of carbon monoxide poisoning?

Symptoms can include headache, dizziness, weakness, nausea, confusion and loss of consciousness. If poisoning is suspected, move everyone outside immediately and call 911. Do not stay inside to investigate or rescue someone without proper equipment.

How far from the house should a generator run?

Operate portable generators outside, at least 20 feet from the building and away from openings, with exhaust directed away from doors, windows and vents. Never run one in a garage or enclosed space, even with doors open.

Can I heat my home with a grill, camp stove, or gas oven?

Never use a grill, camp stove or gas oven to heat a home. Use only appliances approved for the intended indoor location and installed and vented according to their instructions. Opening a window does not make outdoor-only equipment safe indoors.

Do carbon monoxide detectors work without grid power?

Use battery-operated or battery-backed carbon monoxide alarms near sleeping areas and on each level as directed by the manufacturer. Test them regularly. If an alarm sounds, evacuate and call emergency services from outside.
