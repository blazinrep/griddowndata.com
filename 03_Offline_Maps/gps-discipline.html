The blue dot is a luxury. Make the battery last.

GPS is a passive receiver — it listens to satellites, it doesn't transmit — so it works fine in airplane mode, which also disables the cellular and Wi-Fi radios that drain the most power hunting for a signal. Combine that with minimum screen brightness, a dark map theme, and short batch position checks instead of continuous tracking, and a single charge stretches from hours to days.

The rules

  • Airplane mode, GPS on. Cellular and Wi-Fi radios burn power constantly searching for a signal that may not exist. GPS doesn't need either to get a fix.
  • Screen brightness to minimum. The display is usually the single biggest power draw on a phone. Use the lowest brightness you can still read outdoors, and a dark or black map theme if your app supports one.
  • Batch your checks. Turn navigation on, get a position fix, log your coordinates on paper, then power the screen off — rather than leaving the map running continuously in your hand.
  • Dedicate a power bank to navigation only. If you're also using your phone for calls, photos, or a flashlight, that draw competes with navigation. A separate bank reserved for the nav device removes that conflict.
  • Cold drains batteries fast. Lithium batteries lose real capacity in the cold. Keep the device and any spare batteries close to body warmth when not actively in use.
  • Paper is the real backup. Treat GPS as a convenience layered on top of a map and compass, not a replacement for them — batteries die, screens crack, and satellite lock isn't guaranteed.

Frequently asked questions

Does GPS work in airplane mode?

Yes. GPS is a passive receiver — your phone listens to satellite signals, it doesn't transmit to get a position fix. Airplane mode disables the cellular and Wi-Fi radios that drain the most battery scanning for a signal, while GPS keeps working.

How do I make a phone's battery last longer for navigation?

Use airplane mode with GPS/location left on, drop screen brightness to the minimum readable level, switch to a dark map theme, and check your position in short batch sessions rather than leaving navigation running continuously.

Should I rely on GPS as my only navigation method?

No. Treat GPS as a convenience layered on top of a paper map and compass, not a replacement for them. Batteries die, devices break, and satellite signal can be lost — the paper backup doesn't have any of those failure points.
