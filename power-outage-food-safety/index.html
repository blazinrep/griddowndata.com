Power Outage Food Safety: When to Keep or Discard Food

Keep doors closed during outages. An unopened refrigerator generally stays cold for about four hours; a full freezer about 48 hours and a half-full one about 24. Use actual temperature readings and discard unsafe perishables rather than tasting them.

Prepare appliance thermometers, ice and coolers in advance. Check official guidance for individual foods.

Frequently asked questions

How long does food stay safe in the fridge and freezer without power?

With doors closed, a refrigerator generally keeps food cold for four hours; a full freezer for 48 hours and half-full for 24. After four hours without power or another cold source, discard refrigerated perishables unless you can verify they remained safely cold.

What temperature means food is no longer safe?

Keep refrigerated food at 40°F or below. Discard perishables exposed above 40°F for two hours or more (one hour above 90°F ambient temperature). Use the official food-by-food chart when deciding what to discard.

Which foods are safe to refreeze after an outage?

Frozen food that still contains ice crystals or is 40°F or below may generally be refrozen. Use the official chart for exceptions and discard unsafe or uncertain items.

How can I keep food cold longer during a long outage?

Keep doors closed and use coolers with enough ice to maintain 40°F or below. Handle dry ice with insulated gloves, ventilation and manufacturer guidance; never put it in a sealed container.

Can I tell if food has gone bad by smell or taste?

No. Food can contain pathogens without changes in smell, taste or appearance. Do not taste food to test safety; when in doubt, discard it.
