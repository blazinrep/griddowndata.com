Winter Car Emergency Kit and Stranded Driver Safety

A winter vehicle kit should cover warmth, visibility, communication, traction, food and water. If stranded, contact emergency services, share your location and generally stay with the vehicle unless it is unsafe. Prevent carbon monoxide exposure when using the engine.

Check the forecast, avoid travel during dangerous conditions and keep emergency supplies accessible in the cabin.

Frequently asked questions

What should I keep in a winter car emergency kit?

Pack for warmth, visibility, traction, and time: wool or synthetic blankets or a sleeping bag, spare hats, gloves, and socks, chemical hand warmers, bottled water and calorie-dense snacks, a flashlight and headlamp with spare batteries, a charged power bank, a small snow shovel, an ice scraper, sand or cat litter for tire traction, jumper cables, a basic first aid kit, and bright cloth or a flare to flag down help. Keep it inside the cabin, not only the trunk.

Should I stay in my car or walk for help if stranded in a storm?

Call for help and share your location. Stay with the vehicle unless there is immediate danger or rescuers direct you otherwise. Make yourself visible and conserve battery power.

How do I run the car heater safely without carbon monoxide poisoning?

Never run the engine in an enclosed area or with a blocked exhaust. If it is safe, clear snow from around the tailpipe, keep checking it and run the engine briefly (about ten minutes per hour), with a window slightly open. If exhaust may enter the cabin, shut the engine off; ventilation alone is not protection.

How do I stay warm in a stranded vehicle overnight?

Layer up with everything you packed, wrap in blankets or a sleeping bag, and insulate yourself from the cold seat with floor mats or spare clothing. Huddle with other occupants to share body heat, and do light movements like clapping and flexing to keep circulation going. Keep snacks and water within reach, since digesting food helps the body make heat, and avoid alcohol, which feels warming but actually speeds heat loss.

How should I prepare my car before winter driving?

Check tires, battery, coolant and washer fluid, keep fuel available and share your route. Avoid travel if authorities advise staying off roads.
