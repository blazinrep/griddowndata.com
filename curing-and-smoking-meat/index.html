When the freezer dies, salt and smoke are how the meat survives the summer.

Curing and smoking meat is preserving it with salt, and often nitrite and wood smoke, so it keeps for weeks or months without refrigeration. Salt draws out moisture and creates conditions hostile to bacteria, curing salts containing sodium nitrite guard against botulism in low-oxygen cured products, and cold or hot smoking adds antimicrobial compounds and flavor. Frequently asked questions

How does salt preserve meat?

Salt preserves through osmosis: it pulls water out of the meat and out of any microbes present, leaving a dry, high-salt environment where spoilage bacteria cannot grow. Traditional long-keeping cures use heavy salt concentrations, while modern table cures use less and lean on refrigeration or nitrite for safety. The guide explains dry-curing versus wet brining and how much salt each method actually requires.

Why do cured meats need curing salt or nitrite?

Curing salts containing sodium nitrite specifically inhibit Clostridium botulinum, the bacterium that produces deadly botulism toxin in the low-oxygen conditions inside cured and smoked meat. Products like Prague Powder #1 are about 6.25 percent nitrite blended into regular salt. The guide covers correct dosing, why you never eyeball it, and which methods can safely skip nitrite versus which absolutely require it.

What is the difference between cold and hot smoking?

Cold smoking holds meat below roughly 90°F to flavor and help preserve it without cooking, and it depends on a proper cure first because that temperature range otherwise favors bacteria. Hot smoking cooks the meat at 165°F or higher and preserves for a shorter time. The guide explains both, their equipment, and why cold smoking demands the most care and cleanliness.

How long does cured and smoked meat last unrefrigerated?

It depends on the method: heavily salt-cured, dried products like hard salami or country ham can keep for months in a cool, dry place, while lightly smoked meat lasts only days without refrigeration.
